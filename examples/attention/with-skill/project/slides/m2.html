Scaled dot-product attention

Attention(Q, K, V) = softmax(QKᵀ / √dₖ) V

  1. QKᵀ: one score for every pair of words
  1. ÷ √dₖ: keeps the scores small
  1. softmax: turns the scores into weights
  1. × V: the weighted sum of the values
  • Why divide by √dₖ
  • Large dot products leave softmax with very small gradients

Source: Vaswani et al., NIPS 2017, Eq. 1, Sec. 3.2.1. Q, K, V: matrices of queries, keys and values; dₖ: dimension of the keys

[≤ 6:20] Part two is the attention function itself. The authors call it scaled dot-product attention, and the formula on the slide is all of it. Read it from the inside out. All queries are stacked into one matrix, and so are the keys and the values. First the queries are multiplied with the keys, which gives one score for every pair of words. Then every score is divided by the square root of the key dimension. Then a softmax turns the scores into weights. Finally the weights are multiplied with the values. Why the division? With large vectors the dot products become very large. The softmax then has very small gradients, and learning slows down.
