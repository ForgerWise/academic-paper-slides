Source: Vaswani et al., NIPS 2017, Table 3 rows (A), (D), (E), Sec. 6.2

[≤ 10:50] Which parts of the model matter? The authors changed one setting at a time and measured BLEU on the development set. Three findings are on this slide. First, the number of heads. One head is clearly worse than eight, but thirty-two heads is also worse, so more is not always better. Second, dropout. Without dropout the score falls by more than one point. Third, the position vectors. A learned table gives almost the same score as the sine and cosine waves. So the choice of waves is not what makes the model work.
