Method 4

  • Without recurrence the model cannot see word order
  • Positional encoding: a position vector is added to each word embedding
  • The vector is made of sine and cosine waves
  • Wavelengths from 2π to 10000·2π
  • Why waves
  • A fixed shift is a linear function of the encoding, so relative positions are easy to learn
  • May work for sentences longer than those seen in training

Source: Vaswani et al., NIPS 2017, Sec. 3.5

[≤ 7:30] There is one thing missing. Attention treats the sentence as a set, so the model has no idea about word order. The authors fix this at the input. They add a position vector to every word embedding. The position vector is built from sine and cosine waves with many different frequencies. Why waves and not a learned table? The authors give two reasons. With waves, a shift by a fixed number of words is a linear function of the encoding, so relative positions should be easy to learn. And waves may also work for sentences longer than any sentence in the training data.
