Positional Encoding

  • Without recurrence the model cannot see word order
  • Add a position vector to each word
  • Made of sine and cosine waves
  • Why waves
  • Relative positions are easy to learn
  • May extend to longer sentences

Figure: Vaswani et al., NIPS 2017, Fig. 1, Sec. 3.5; spacing widened and orange box added by presenter

[≤ 8:23] Part C. One thing is still missing. Attention treats the sentence as a set, so the model has no idea about word order. The authors fix this at the input, and the orange box in the figure shows where. They add a position vector to every word embedding. The position vector is built from sine and cosine waves with many different frequencies. Why waves and not a learned table? With waves, a shift by a fixed number of words is a linear function of the encoding, so relative positions should be easy to learn. And waves may also work for sentences longer than any sentence in the training data.
